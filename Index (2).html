💥 Supernova Rebirth & Shield Lab · v1.2
Remnant gas / dust yield1.0×
Shield effectiveness (fictional)85%
Select a star with at least one nearby planet to deploy a shield.
Explosions seed colorful remnant clouds and a bounded debris ring around the neutron star or black hole. Remnant clouds can form a second generation of stars. Shields are science-fiction technology: they reduce our game's pulsar, magnetar, quasar and supernova radiation damage, but consume charge. Real gas shells or magnetic fields would not reliably stop gamma rays, and ordinary radio waves are generally not hazardous.
🌟 Scale & Quasi-Star Lab · v1.3
Object size display
Relative sizes show a compressed hierarchy, not real astronomical scale. Gravity and collision radii are unchanged.
Select Quasi-Star, then click space to spawn one. Transform an existing star using Stellar Forge.
A quasi-star is a hypothetical early-universe object: an enormous gas envelope powered by accretion onto a black hole at its center. No confirmed quasi-stars have been observed. Collapse Core exposes a feeding black hole with a bounded accretion disk and possible jets; it does not explode into a galaxy. Size modes are visual-only compressed comparisons, not a literal common scale; all orbits and collisions keep their original toy physics radii.
🌌 Galactic Evolution Lab · v1.4

A dense group of 30 real simulated stars is recognized as a cluster. With nearby nebula gas it grows a visual protogalaxy over time; stars never instantly become a galaxy.
30 is a simulation trigger, not a real galaxy threshold. Real galaxies contain vastly more stars and assemble through gas accretion, dark-matter gravity and mergers. Growing halos use decorative stars, not additional N-body masses.

🔥 Cosmic Plate · v1.5
Select a black hole. Feed it 5 red supergiants by collisions or complete tidal disruptions to unlock a gigantic attached red/orange/white disk.
After five red supergiant meals, the black hole's gas disk is drawn as one smoothly animated structure, not thousands of particles. Further red supergiants make it wider. The plate follows the black hole and survives mergers/save files. This is a stylized rendering of orbiting gas, not a solid object or a real astrophysical five-star threshold.

Select a star or black hole, then Create Disk. Stars get warm protoplanetary-style rings that can turn into orbiting protoplanets! Auto-formation needs at least 60 gas particles, consumes a local dust clump, and stops at 5 planets per star / 40 total massive bodies. Black-hole disks stay gas-only. This is an illustrative toy model: particles have no self-gravity and newly formed planet mass is not conserved. 2,500-particle cap.

Life advances from microbes → complex life → civilization after sustained suitable conditions. Multiple suns contribute heat; stellar explosions can cause extinctions. 🌀 Extreme Objects Lab · v0.8
Radiation intensity (gameplay)1.0×
Select a neutron star (or any star) to turn it into a pulsar or magnetar. Select a black hole to switch its quasar jets on/off. Rotating pulsar beams, magnetar fields and quasar jets can irradiate nearby planets over time; atmospheres offer partial protection. Use the hazard checkbox for a safe visual-only mode. These are stylized effects, not physical magnetohydrodynamics or relativistic jets.
🕳️🕳️ Black Hole Merger Lab · v0.9
Inspiral strength (gameplay)1.0×
Black hole mergers0
Waiting for a black hole binary…
Create two black holes in orbit, or use the binary preset without clearing your galaxy. Close pairs lose orbital energy and spiral inward. On contact they merge, disturb nearby disk particles, emit expanding visual wavefronts and may receive a recoil kick. The inspiral, wave rings and kick are stylized gameplay approximations, not a general-relativity calculation. The rings do not damage life.
Overlay Elements
Select a star, black hole or planet to place stable-distance orbit guides.
Fixed-distance orbit guides: 5 inner, 2 habitable, 4 outer for stars; black holes have inner/middle/outer zones (not habitable). Select a host, choose Orbit, and click a guide. Moon guides stay within a conservative Hill-sphere fraction. Guides are cached so they do not shift unexpectedly; use Recalibrate if the star evolves. Magnetic fields are enlarged visualizations, not realistic physical boundaries or collision forces. Multi-body orbits can still become unstable.
